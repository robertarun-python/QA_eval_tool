Score these five areas, each 0-20 (total 100), using PRIMARY EVIDENCE only:

1. automation_design (0-20) - does the code faithfully automate the candidate's OWN design? Right flow, reuses the provided helpers rather than reinventing them, structured so it could be run repeatedly.

3. ai_usage (0-20) - were the prompts specific, grounded in their own design, and appropriately scoped? Asking the assistant to encode a stated step is good use. The AI must not receive credit here for inventing an important automation decision (a case, a data value, an assertion, a design choice) that the candidate never specified or reviewed - that is a weakness in this area, not a strength.
4. ai_output_review (0-20) - is there evidence the candidate actually reviewed what the assistant produced rather than accepting it wholesale? Look for their own edits, corrections, follow-ups that catch a problem, or a rejected suggestion. Accepting a correct suggestion unchanged is acceptable; never examining anything is not.

A candidate who notices their test passed for the wrong reason scores well here. A candidate who declares success on a run that didn't prove their requirement scores poorly, even if the run exited cleanly. Execution PASS alone is not proof of correct automation, and running the code is not by itself evidence of strong automation engineering - weigh it together with what the design, data/assertions and review evidence above actually show.
